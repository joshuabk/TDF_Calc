BED — Linear-Quadratic Gap Compensation

Solves for the make-up needed to restore the prescribed tumor BED after a treatment gap — by adding fractions at the same dose per fraction, by raising the dose per fraction over the fractions still owed so no treatment days are added, or by giving those fractions BID — and shows what each schedule does to early- and late-responding normal tissue. Treatment is assumed Monday–Friday; the finish date follows from the fraction count. Dose in Gy.

Model

BEDnormal = N · d · (1 + d / (α/β)) − K · T

Eq. (A): N = number of well-spaced fractions, d = dose per fraction (Gy).

BEDtumor = N · d · (1 + d / (α/β)) − K · (T − Tk)   for T ≥ Tk

Eq. (B): T = overall treatment time in calendar days (weekends and the gap count, since repopulation continues on days without treatment), Tk = lag before rapid repopulation, K = daily BED-equivalent of repopulation. Fractions are given Monday–Friday only; finish dates skip weekends.

Add-fractions solver. Post-gap fractions N are stepped up from 0 until tumor LQ(delivered + N post-gap fx) − K·(Toverall − Tk) reaches BEDprescribed, where Toverall runs from the first fraction to the N-th post-gap fraction; of that N and N − 1, the one whose tumor BED is nearer the prescription is reported. For each N the finish date is the date of the N-th fraction at the chosen fractions per week, so adding a fraction also adds the repopulation cost of the extra calendar days. Fractions to add = N − (prescribed fx − delivered fx).

Dose-per-fraction solver (also used for BID). N is fixed at the fractions still owed (prescribed fx − delivered fx), so the finish date and post-gap days Tpost are fixed too. The post-gap dose d solves N·d·(1 + d/(α/β)) = BEDprescribed − LQdelivered + K·(Toverall − Tk) (positive root of the quadratic), rounded up to the nearest cGy so the delivered tumor BED is never below prescription.
