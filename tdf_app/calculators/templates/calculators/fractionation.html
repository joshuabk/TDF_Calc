Fx Adjust — Same Tumor BED, Different Fractionation

Adjusts the number of fractions or the dose per fraction of a schedule, holding the tumor BED the same with repopulation included: a shorter course loses less to repopulation and needs less dose, a longer one needs more. Shows what each schedule does to early- and late-responding normal tissue. Both courses are uninterrupted and treated Monday–Friday. Dose in Gy.

Model

BED = N · d · (1 + d / (α/β)) − K · (T − Tk)   for T ≥ Tk

N = number of fractions, d = dose per fraction (Gy), T = overall time in calendar days from the first to the last fraction (weekends count), Tk = lag before rapid repopulation, K = daily BED-equivalent of repopulation. T follows from the fraction count: Monday–Friday from the chosen starting weekday, one or two fractions per treatment day.

Dose-per-fraction solver. For the chosen N, T is fixed, and d solves N·d·(1 + d/(α/β)) = BEDoriginal + K·(T − Tk) (positive root of the quadratic), rounded up to the nearest cGy so the tumor BED is never below the original.

Fraction solver. For the chosen d, N is stepped up from 1 until N·d·(1 + d/(α/β)) − K·(TN − Tk) reaches BEDoriginal; each added fraction also adds the repopulation cost of the extra calendar days.

A course that finishes before Tk has no repopulation term. The linear-quadratic model is less reliable at large doses per fraction; a warning is shown above 10 Gy.
